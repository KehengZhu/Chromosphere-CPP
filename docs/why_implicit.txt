We split the source terms because they have **very different numerical stiffness**.

In your writeup, the explicit residual (R_E) contains the MUSCL/Rusanov flux divergence plus the pressure-area and gravity sources, while the implicit residual (R_I) contains ion-neutral drag, collisional heating/temperature exchange, and heat conduction. That split is shown in the conservative-vector scheme: (R_E) is defined with the flux difference plus (S_E), and (S_E) only contains pressure-area and gravity; (R_I) then contains drag, collisional energy exchange, and conductive terms. 

The reason is not that explicit terms are “less important.” It is that they are **less stiff**.

### Why pressure-area and gravity are explicit

The pressure-area term and gravity term look like ordinary force/source terms:

[
pB\partial_s\left(\frac{1}{B}\right),
\qquad
-\rho\partial_s\phi_g .
]

They change momentum on roughly dynamical timescales: sound-crossing, flow, gravity/free-fall, stratification. These are usually comparable to the hyperbolic CFL timestep. So if your timestep is already chosen from

[
\Delta t \lesssim C_{\rm CFL}\frac{\Delta s}{|v|+c_s},
]

then pressure-area and gravity are not usually the limiting stability problem.

Also, the pressure-area source is tied to the flux divergence. The writeup shows that the flux term plus the geometric source together recover the ordinary pressure-gradient force.  Keeping both in the explicit hyperbolic/geometric update is consistent and simple.

### Why drag, thermal exchange, and conduction are implicit

These terms can evolve on much shorter timescales than acoustic motion.

For drag,

[
\lambda_{\rm drag}
==================

\alpha\left(\frac{1}{\rho_i}+\frac{1}{\rho_n}\right).
]

If

[
\Delta t\lambda_{\rm drag}\gg 1,
]

then explicit time stepping is unstable. Your writeup even states that at Model C7 conditions (\alpha\Delta t\sim 60), so the hyperbolic timestep is far too large for explicit drag. 

For heat conduction, the explicit stability condition is roughly

[
\Delta t
\lesssim
\frac{C,\Delta s^2}{K}.
]

That can be much smaller than the hyperbolic CFL timestep, especially near the transition region where electron conductivity grows strongly with temperature. The writeup notes that large (\kappa_e\propto T_e^{5/2}) can make the conduction rate very stiff. 

So those terms are put in (R_I) because otherwise the code would need an extremely tiny timestep.

---

## Could it all be explicit?

Yes, mathematically. You could write

[
U^{n+1}
=======

U^n
+
\Delta t
\left[
R_E(U^n)+R_I(U^n)
\right].
]

That would be much simpler.

But then your timestep must satisfy **all** stability limits:

[
\Delta t
<
\min
\left[
\Delta t_{\rm CFL},
\frac{1}{\lambda_{\rm drag}},
\frac{1}{\lambda_{\rm temp}},
\frac{C\Delta s^2}{K}
\right].
]

In this problem, the drag and conduction limits can be much smaller than the acoustic CFL limit. So an all-explicit method would probably be stable only with a timestep tens, hundreds, or more times smaller. It would be simple but very slow.

---

## Could it all be implicit?

Also yes, in principle. You could solve

[
U^{n+1}
=======

U^n
+
\Delta t
\left[
R_E(U^{n+1})+R_I(U^{n+1})
\right].
]

That would be a fully implicit backward-Euler method.

But it is much harder:

You would need to solve a large nonlinear system coupling all cells and all variables. The fluxes depend on reconstructed primitive variables, the Rusanov wave speeds, pressure, energy, density, and velocities. The source terms also depend on temperatures, conductivities, collision rates, and neighboring-cell conduction. So the Jacobian becomes complicated.

It also adds unnecessary numerical diffusion. Fully implicit hyperbolic solvers are often very robust but can smear waves unless carefully designed. For shocks or waves, explicit Godunov-type finite-volume methods are usually preferred because they are accurate and natural under the CFL condition.

So all-implicit is possible, but it is overkill for this code.

---

## Why IMEX is the usual compromise

The split is an **IMEX strategy**:

[
\text{nonstiff hyperbolic/geometric terms}
\rightarrow
\text{explicit},
]

[
\text{stiff relaxation/diffusion terms}
\rightarrow
\text{implicit}.
]

This gives you the best practical compromise:

[
\Delta t \text{ is chosen by wave propagation, not by collisions or diffusion.}
]

So the purpose is:

**treat fast-but-stable-to-solve-local/global stiff physics implicitly, while keeping the wave-transport part explicit and simple.**

For this model, the split makes sense:

| Term                 | Suggested treatment | Reason                                     |
| -------------------- | ------------------: | ------------------------------------------ |
| MUSCL/Rusanov flux   |            explicit | hyperbolic CFL already controls it         |
| pressure-area source |            explicit | geometric part of pressure-gradient update |
| gravity              |            explicit | usually not the stiffest term              |
| ion-neutral drag     |            implicit | very stiff local relaxation                |
| temperature exchange |            implicit | stiff local thermal relaxation             |
| heat conduction      |            implicit | parabolic timestep limit can be severe     |

So the short answer is: **yes, it can all be explicit or all implicit, but neither is attractive. All explicit is too slow or unstable; all implicit is much more complicated and more diffusive. The mixed explicit/implicit split is used because only some source terms are stiff enough to need implicit treatment.**
